How to prompt for cell structures

AI generators — whether image or 3D — respond best to prompts built from four layers: subject, form, material, and style. Cell organelles have well-defined canonical forms, so anchoring the prompt in biology gives the model the clearest signal.

The four layers

  1. Subject — name the organelle and its biological role in one phrase. "mitochondrion, energy-producing organelle". This sets the semantic target.
  2. Form — describe the 3D shape: cross-section vs. exterior, symmetry, inner structures. "bean-shaped, double membrane, folded cristae inside".
  3. Material — surface quality and translucency. Biological membranes are thin lipid bilayers; they read best as semi-transparent or waxy. "translucent phospholipid bilayer, subsurface scattering".
  4. Style — the visual register. For educational use, "scientific illustration" or "textbook diagram, 3D render" steers away from fantasy interpretations. For VR assets, add "game-ready low-poly" or "stylised, smooth surfaces".
Key rule: every prompt here ends with a negative: --no nucleus, no cell wall, no bacteria (or the equivalent negative-prompt field). Without it, generators frequently add a nucleus to every structure they can't otherwise fill.

Cross-section vs. exterior

Most organelles are only meaningful when seen in cross-section — the outer surface of a mitochondrion is a plain oval. Add "cut-away cross-section view, interior visible" for any structure with important internal anatomy (nucleus, mitochondria, Golgi, ER). Use exterior view only for ribosomes and vesicles.

Scale anchoring

Generators have no innate sense of biological scale. Mention a reference: "approximately 1–10 micrometres" or "smaller than the nucleus, larger than a ribosome". This helps maintain correct relative proportions when you assemble a full cell.

Colour

Scientific convention uses false colour to distinguish structures. Pick a palette before you start and lock each organelle to a hue. The prompts below use the colour palette from the CellXR animal-cell model. Swap to your own palette by replacing the colour description — keep hue, saturation, and the word "matte" or "translucent" to control specular response.

Organelle prompts

Each card has an image prompt (Midjourney / DALL·E / SD) and a 3D prompt (Meshy / Tripo3D). Copy the one that matches your builder. The Negative line is the negative-prompt field for SD; append --no <terms> for Midjourney.

Cell Membrane

plasma membrane

The outer boundary of the animal cell — a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. Irregular, slightly wavy, and translucent. No cell wall.

Image prompt
animal cell plasma membrane, phospholipid bilayer cross-section, irregular wavy organic surface, semi-transparent rose-pink membrane, embedded glycoproteins visible, scientific 3D render, subsurface scattering, dark background, isolated, textbook diagram style, photorealistic materials
3D prompt (Meshy / Tripo3D)
3D model of an animal cell plasma membrane, open curved shell shape like a bowl or hollow sphere with uneven organic edges, semi-transparent pink, PBR translucent material, smooth low-poly stylised, game asset
Negative (SD / Midjourney --no)
cell wall, rigid, bacteria, nucleus, organelles, green, plant cell

Nucleus

control centre

Mitochondria

powerhouse of the cell

protein factory

Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum

lipid synthesis

A network of curved, branching membrane tubules — no ribosomes on the surface. Synthesises lipids and hormones. More tubular and looping than rough ER.

Golgi Apparatus

shipping department

A stack of curved, flattened membrane sacs (cisternae) — like a stack of deflated balloons. Has a cis face (receiving from ER) and a trans face (shipping vesicles).

Ribosomes

protein synthesis

Tiny two-subunit complexes — a large and a small subunit fitting together. Appear as dense dots ≈ 25 nm diameter. Occur free in the cytoplasm or bound to rough ER.

Vacuole

storage compartment

In animal cells, vacuoles are small and numerous — temporary storage sacs for water, ions, or waste. Single membrane (tonoplast). More irregular than a lysosome.

Refinement tips

If the shape is wrong

Add a reference to a familiar object: "Golgi apparatus shaped like a stack of pita breads", "mitochondrion shaped like a kidney bean". Everyday analogies outperform pure biology terminology for shape control.

If the colour bleeds

Specify the colour as an exclusive: "only deep purple, no green, no red". In Midjourney append --no green --no red. In SDXL put the unwanted colour in the negative prompt at weight 1.4.

If the material looks plastic

Add "subsurface scattering, biological tissue, translucent lipid bilayer, thin membrane". For 3D builders, request "PBR transmission material, IOR 1.45".

Testing across builders

Run each image prompt in Midjourney and DALL·E first. Pick the rendering that best matches the anatomy. Feed that image as a reference into Meshy or Tripo3D for the 3D step. This image-to-3D pipeline consistently produces better geometry than pure text-to-3D.

Assembling the full cell

Generate each organelle at a consistent relative scale. Use the nucleus as your anchor — it should be roughly 5–10× the diameter of a single mitochondrion. Bring all assets into Blender, scale to match, then export one GLB. The CellXR Annotation Helper can label the parts and generate the grouping JSON for the 3D viewer.
